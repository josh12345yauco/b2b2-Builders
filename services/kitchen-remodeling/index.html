[ An Honest Breakdown ]

Refresh, Remodel, or Gut?

Most of the price of a kitchen isn’t the pretty parts — it’s what moves. Here’s how we explain it across the kitchen table:

  1. 01

    A cosmetic refresh

    Keeps the layout: new counters, new doors or paint on the cabinets, new backsplash, new fixtures. If your layout works and the boxes are solid, this is real money saved and a one-to-two-week job.

  2. 02

    A pull-and-replace remodel

    Keeps plumbing and gas where they are but replaces everything: cabinets, counters, flooring, lighting, tile. The most common Philadelphia kitchen project and the best value-per-dollar for most homes.

  3. 03

    A layout change or full gut

    Where cost jumps — and it’s rarely the cabinets. Moving the sink means re-running supply and drain lines; moving a gas range means a new permitted gas line; removing a rowhome wall usually means a structural beam. It just needs to be priced honestly up front instead of discovered in a change order.

  4. 04

    The island question

    Every kitchen wants an island; not every rowhome can seat one. A working island needs 42 inches of clearance on its working sides, and in a 14-foot-wide rowhome that math is tight. We’ll tell you honestly whether your kitchen fits an island, a peninsula, or a smarter run of counter — before you fall in love with a rendering.

[ Rowhome Reality ]

Built on Bones That Can Carry It.

We check joist condition and stiffness before promising a tile floor — cheap insurance against cracked grout lines two winters later.

Older Philadelphia kitchens often sit on floor framing that was never meant for porcelain tile and stone counters. Sistering or reinforcing old joists while the floor is open costs little; discovering bouncy framing after the tile cracks costs a second remodel.

It’s the same instinct everywhere in these houses: open it up, look honestly at what’s there, and price what the room actually needs — not just what the brochure shows.

[ Questions ]

Kitchen Remodel Answers.

Do I need a permit to remodel a kitchen in Philadelphia?

If we're moving plumbing, changing electrical circuits, or touching structure — yes, and we pull it. Philadelphia L&I requires permits and licensed-trade sign-offs for that work, and unpermitted work surfaces at resale. Cosmetic swaps like painting, new counters on existing cabinets, or replacing a faucet generally don't need one. We handle all permitting as part of the job.

How long does a kitchen remodel take?

A straightforward pull-and-replace kitchen typically runs 3 to 5 weeks once materials are on site. A full gut with layout changes runs 6 to 10 weeks. The schedule is in your proposal before we start, and cabinet lead times are the most common variable — we order early for that reason.

Can I live in my house during the remodel?

Almost everyone does. We seal off the work area with dust barriers, protect floors along the path to the door, and keep a working water source available. For kitchens, most clients set up a temporary station with the fridge and microwave in another room. If your home has only one bathroom, we plan the plumbing switchover so you're never without a working toilet overnight.

Should I buy my own fixtures and materials?

You can, and some clients enjoy it. The trade-off: anything you supply, you're responsible for — sizing, delivery timing, damage, and returns. When we supply materials, we warranty the whole installation, wrong parts are our problem, and our trade pricing usually offsets any retail sale you'd find. Either way works; we just want it decided before the schedule is built.

What's the difference between stock, semi-custom, and custom cabinets?

Stock cabinets come in fixed sizes and limited finishes — the budget option, fine for rentals and simple layouts. Semi-custom gives you more sizes, depths, and finish choices, and is the sweet spot for most Philadelphia homes. Full custom is built to your exact space — the answer for out-of-square old rowhomes, ceilings over nine feet, or a look you can't get from a catalog.

Is your remodeling work warrantied?

Yes. Our workmanship is covered in writing, and manufacturer warranties on cabinets, fixtures, and appliances pass through to you with the paperwork at closeout. If a door sags or a caulk line fails, you call us and we fix it.
