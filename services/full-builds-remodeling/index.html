[ The Real Choice ]

Gut Renovation vs. New Construction.

In Philadelphia this is the decision that shapes everything downstream — budget, timeline, permits, and risk. Here’s what you’re really choosing between:

  1. 01

    A gut renovation keeps the shell

    Usually the brick side walls, sometimes the facade — and replaces everything inside. Often the smart play here, because old rowhome shells are frequently the best part of the building, and keeping them can simplify zoning. The catch: you don’t fully know what you have until demo opens the walls. We de-risk that with a thorough pre-construction inspection and realistic allowances — surprises become priced categories, not blank checks. If you’re weighing it, read our guide to gut renovation vs. cosmetic remodel.

  2. 02

    New construction trades demo risk for site work

    Everything is known, plumb, square, and insulated to modern code — but the timeline runs through zoning, full permit review, and excavation. On tight streets, infill construction also means party-wall agreements, protection of adjacent foundations, and staging logistics an out-of-town builder will underestimate.

  3. 03

    What’s usually hiding in an old rowhome

    Joists notched by eighty years of plumbers, knob-and-tube wiring under blown-in insulation, galvanized supply lines pretending to be full-pressure, chimneys that vent nothing but still carry framing loads. We plan for all of it — and when a client is deciding between a shell and a finished flip, we’ll walk it with them before they buy.

[ Questions ]

Full Build Answers.

How long does a full gut renovation take in Philadelphia?

A typical rowhome gut renovation runs 4 to 6 months of construction after permits. Ground-up new construction runs 8 to 12 months including the permit and foundation phases. Permit approval time through L&I varies with the scope — that's why we file early and tell you the realistic date, not the hopeful one.

Do you handle the permits and inspections?

Yes, all of it — building permits through Philadelphia L&I or the local township, licensed-trade permits for electrical and plumbing, scheduled inspections at each phase, and the certificate of occupancy at the end. You never stand in line at the Municipal Services Building.

What's usually hiding inside an old rowhome?

The common finds: undersized or notched joists, knob-and-tube wiring, galvanized or lead water lines, dead chimneys carrying floor loads, and party walls that need repointing. None of these should be surprises priced as change orders — an experienced builder expects them, inspects for them up front, and carries realistic allowances in the proposal.

Can you work from my architect's plans?

Yes. We regularly build from architects' and engineers' drawings, and we're happy to be brought in during design — pricing input at the drawing stage saves most clients real money before anything is locked in. If you don't have an architect yet, we can recommend ones we've built with.

Do you do additions and top-floor add-ons?

Yes — rear additions, second-floor additions over one-story kitchens, and top-floor additions with roof decks where zoning allows. These are structure-heavy projects that live or die on the framing and the connection to the existing building, which is core work for us.

How do payments work on a big project?

A capped deposit to secure the schedule and order long-lead materials, then milestone payments tied to completed phases — never big sums ahead of the work. Every payment matches a line in the written schedule you approved.
