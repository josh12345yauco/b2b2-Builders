[ An Honest Breakdown ]

Refresh, Remodel, or Gut?

Most of the price of a bathroom isn’t the pretty parts — it’s what moves, and what has to never leak. Here’s how we explain it:

  1. 01

    A cosmetic refresh

    Keeps the layout: new vanity, toilet, fixtures, lighting, paint, and re-caulking. If the tile and tub are sound, this is real money saved and a one-week job that makes the room feel new.

  2. 02

    A pull-and-replace remodel

    Tub, toilet, and vanity stay where they are, but everything gets replaced — new tile, new fixtures, new waterproofing behind it all. The most common Philadelphia bath project and the best value-per-dollar.

  3. 03

    A layout change or full gut

    Where cost jumps. Moving fixtures off the wet wall means re-running supply and drain lines — in a rowhome, often through a century-old cast iron stack that deserves replacing while the walls are open.

  4. 04

    The one-bath rowhome

    Half of Philadelphia lives with a single bathroom. We sequence the plumbing switchover so you’re never without a working toilet overnight, and we compress the no-shower window as tightly as tile cure times allow.

[ Waterproofing First ]

The Part You’ll Never See Matters Most.

Tile doesn’t keep water out of your walls — the waterproofing behind it does.

Every wet wall we build gets a real membrane system behind the tile, a shower pan tested before it’s covered, and ventilation actually sized to the room — because the failures that ruin bathrooms are the ones you can’t see until the ceiling below stains.

While the walls are open we also look hard at what’s inside them — joists notched by old plumbers, galvanized supply lines, a cast iron stack on its last decade. Fixing them mid-remodel costs little; finding them after the tile is up costs the remodel twice.

[ Questions ]

Bath Remodel Answers.

Do I need a permit to remodel a bathroom in Philadelphia?

If we're moving plumbing, changing electrical circuits, or touching structure — yes, and we pull it. Philadelphia L&I requires permits and licensed-trade sign-offs for that work, and unpermitted work surfaces at resale. Cosmetic swaps like a new vanity, faucet, or paint generally don't need one. We handle all permitting as part of the job.

How long does a bathroom remodel take?

A pull-and-replace bathroom typically runs 2 to 4 weeks once materials are on site. A full gut with layout changes, a curbless shower conversion, or stack work runs 4 to 6 weeks. The schedule is in your proposal before we start, and tile and glass lead times are the most common variable — we order early for that reason.

My house has only one bathroom — can you still remodel it?

Yes — it's one of the most common Philadelphia rowhome situations, and we plan for it. We sequence the plumbing switchover so you're never without a working toilet overnight, and we compress the no-shower window as tightly as the tile cure times allow. You'll know the exact plan before demo day.

Should I convert my tub to a walk-in shower?

If it's your only bathroom, we usually recommend keeping at least one tub in the house for resale — families with young kids look for one. If you have two baths, converting the primary to a curbless walk-in shower is one of the most-requested upgrades we do, and it makes the room feel dramatically bigger.

Are heated bathroom floors worth it?

In a Philadelphia rowhome with a bathroom over an unheated space or on an exterior wall — genuinely yes. Electric radiant mats add modest cost while the floor is already open, run on a programmable thermostat, and are the single upgrade clients tell us they'd never give up.

Is your remodeling work warrantied?

Yes. Our workmanship is covered in writing, and manufacturer warranties on fixtures, glass, and vanities pass through to you with the paperwork at closeout. If a caulk line fails or a door drifts, you call us and we fix it.
