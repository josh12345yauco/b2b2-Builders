Building in Northern Liberties

Northern Liberties rebuilt itself before most of the city caught on, which means its housing stock skews new by Philadelphia standards: townhome rows from the 2000s building boom, converted industrial buildings with concrete floors and steel windows, and condo buildings with associations that have opinions. The projects here are different from the gut-and-rebuild work we do deeper in the River Wards — and so is the skill set.

Townhome work in NoLibs is often about correcting the boom's shortcuts: builder-grade kitchens and baths that deserved better, roof decks reaching the end of their membrane's life, and pilot houses that leak where the flashing met the framing. We remodel the interiors to the standard the neighborhood now expects and rebuild the decks so they stop being an annual maintenance argument.

The loft conversions are their own animal — thick masonry walls, exposed structure, and mechanical systems threaded through spaces that were never meant to hide them. Renovating one well means working with the industrial character instead of drywalling over it.

Resale expectations shape scope here too. Northern Liberties buyers have seen a decade of well-finished units, and appraisals reward finish quality in a way some neighborhoods don't yet. That changes the renovation math: the difference between builder-grade and genuinely custom work often comes back at sale, which is why our NoLibs clients tend to choose the better tile and the real millwork once they see both numbers side by side. We'll show you where the upgrade pays and where it's just expensive.

And everywhere in this neighborhood, logistics are part of the build. Deliveries get staged around narrow one-way streets, dumpster permits get timed, associations get certificates of insurance before anyone lifts a tool, and quiet hours get respected because your neighbors share a wall — or a floor. We plan that coordination into the schedule up front, which is why our NoLibs timelines hold.

Northern Liberties Answers.

How much does a townhome remodel cost in Northern Liberties?

Most Northern Liberties kitchen and bathroom remodels land between $25,000 and $60,000, whole-home refreshes of boom-era townhomes typically run $80,000 to $200,000, and roof deck rebuilds usually start around $20,000. Every estimate is free, itemized, and based on walking your actual home.

Do you serve Northern Liberties?

Yes — it's ten minutes down Frankford Ave from our headquarters, and we work there regularly. Townhome remodels, condo interiors, roof decks, and kitchen and bath renovations are our most common Northern Liberties projects.

Can you work with our HOA or condo association?

Yes, and we're used to it. Northern Liberties runs on associations — we provide certificates of insurance, follow the building's work rules and hours, coordinate elevator and dumpster logistics, and get the association's sign-offs before work starts so your project doesn't stall in a board email chain.

Do you build roof decks and pilot houses?

Yes. Roof decks with pilot-house access are one of the neighborhood's signature projects — structure, waterproofing, zoning, and framing all have to agree. We handle the full scope, from permits to the last deck board.
