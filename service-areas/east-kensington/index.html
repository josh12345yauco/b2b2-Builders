Building in East Kensington

East Kensington's story is written in its vacancy patterns: blocks where three renovated rowhomes stand next to two sealed shells and an empty lot. For a builder, that's not blight — it's the raw material of the neighborhood's next chapter, and it's work we know intimately. Our ground-up modern custom home put a fully custom build on one of those blocks, and our signature rowhome rehab turned a tired property on the Fishtown border into one of the most distinctive houses on its street.

Rehabbing a long-vacant rowhome is a different discipline from remodeling a lived-in one. Vacancy is hard on buildings: roofs fail quietly for years, water finds the joist ends, copper walks away, and what's left behind the plaster is usually nothing you'd want to keep. So we don't keep it. The East Kensington playbook is full systems replacement — new electrical service and wiring, new supply and waste plumbing, new HVAC, new roof — inside a repaired and repointed brick shell. It sounds drastic; it's actually the honest path, because half-measures in a building like this just relocate the problem behind new drywall.

East Kensington also pairs rehab work with new construction more than any neighborhood we serve — the same block often hands us both. Empty lots between rowhomes become infill builds, which means new foundations poured tight against century-old party walls, underpinning coordination, and framing that ties politely into structures built when Roosevelt was president. The first one. Having concrete, framing, and finish crews under one roof is what makes those hybrid blocks workable on one schedule.

The other half of the work here is knowing what the city requires to bring a vacant property back to legal occupancy: full L&I permitting, licensed-trade sign-offs, inspections at every phase, and a certificate of occupancy at the end. Investors feel this in their carrying costs — a permit path that stalls is a loan that keeps accruing. We file early and sequence the build so inspections never become the critical path.

East Kensington Answers.

How much does it cost to renovate a shell in East Kensington?

A full shell rehab with complete systems replacement typically runs $150,000 to $300,000 depending on size and structure, and standalone kitchens land between $25,000 and $60,000. Every estimate is free, itemized, and priced from walking the actual building — with allowances for what vacancy usually hides.

Do you work with investors in East Kensington?

Yes — a large share of our East Kensington work is investor rehabs: shells and long-vacant rowhomes brought back to code for rental or resale. We speak both languages: the homeowner's finish standards and the investor's budget-per-door math.

Do you handle permits for shell renovations?

Yes. A shell rehab in East Kensington typically needs full L&I permitting with licensed electrical and plumbing sign-offs, since everything behind the walls is being replaced. We file it all and manage each inspection — on a vacant-property rehab, clean permitting is also what protects your resale.

What condition are East Kensington shells usually in?

Honestly? Rough — and that's fine. Years of vacancy mean water damage, failed roofs, stripped systems, and sometimes fire history. The brick shells are usually still worth saving. We assess structure first, price the full systems replacement realistically, and tell you plainly when a shell is a teardown instead.
