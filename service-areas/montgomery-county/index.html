Building in Montgomery County

Cross the county line and the whole geometry of construction changes. In the city we build up because there's nowhere else to go; in Ambler, Blue Bell, and Conshohocken, houses have side yards, back yards, and setbacks — so projects grow outward. Additions are the signature Montgomery County job: family rooms off the kitchen, primary-suite wings, second stories over garages. For a crew that spends its city days engineering around party walls, a detached house on a real lot is honest luxury — access on four sides, staging space, and room for the excavator.

The paperwork geography changes too, and this trips up city contractors more than the construction does. There is no L&I out here. Ambler is its own borough with its own permit office; Blue Bell falls under Whitpain Township; Conshohocken is a borough wrapped by Whitemarsh and Plymouth townships — and each authority has its own forms, review pace, inspectors, and opinions. Zoning matters differently as well: setback and lot-coverage rules shape what an addition can be before design even starts. We identify the right authority for your address on day one and build its actual timeline — not a city timeline — into the schedule.

Additions out here also let families stay put while we build — usually the whole point of the project. We frame and weather-in the new structure before opening the wall between old and new, so the house stays sealed, heated, and livable until the last practical moment. The break-through, when it comes, is measured in days, not months. It's the difference between "we added on while living there and it was fine" and the horror stories your neighbors tell — and it's a sequencing decision, made on day one.

The building stock rewards the same judgment we use in the city, differently applied: mid-century ranches and split-levels with paneled basements waiting to be finished properly, 1990s colonials with builder-grade kitchens due for their first real remodel, and older borough homes in Ambler and Conshohocken's walkable cores that mix stone foundations with a century of amateur wiring. Different patients, same diagnostic habits.

Montgomery County Answers.

How much does a home addition cost in Montgomery County?

Additions in Ambler, Blue Bell, and Conshohocken typically run $150 to $300 per square foot depending on scope and finishes, and kitchen remodels land between $30,000 and $75,000. Every estimate is free, itemized, and includes the township permit path in the schedule.

Which Montgomery County towns do you serve?

Our regular coverage is Ambler, Blue Bell, and Conshohocken, plus the surrounding townships along that corridor. If your project is a good fit slightly beyond that range, call — for full builds and additions we'll travel a little farther.

Do you handle township permits outside Philadelphia?

Yes. Outside the city, permits run through each municipality rather than Philadelphia L&I — Ambler borough and the surrounding townships each have their own process, inspectors, and timelines. We identify the right authority for your address, file, and manage inspections through final approval.

What kinds of projects do you do in Montgomery County?

Additions are the headline — family rooms, primary suites, and second stories on homes with real lots. Behind those: kitchen and bathroom remodels, basement finishing, and full builds. Detached homes and twins give us room to work that rowhomes don't, and the projects take advantage of it.
