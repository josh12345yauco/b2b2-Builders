Building in Fishtown

Fishtown is two neighborhoods wearing one name. On the same block you'll find a brick rowhome built when the shad fishery was still an industry and a four-story infill build finished last spring. We work on both — and the skill sets are completely different.

The old housing stock here is roughly a century old, and gut renovations are the neighborhood's daily rhythm. That means opening up walls that have never been opened: joists notched by generations of plumbers, knob-and-tube wiring, and party walls whose brick needs repointing while everything's exposed. Structural surprises aren't surprises in Fishtown — they're line items an experienced builder expects and prices honestly. When clients want more headroom downstairs, we underpin basements — engineered, permitted work on shared walls that we treat with the seriousness two attached houses deserve.

The new-construction side runs through Philadelphia L&I: building permits, licensed-trade sign-offs, and inspections at every phase. Top-floor additions with roof decks are the neighborhood's signature move — structure-heavy projects where zoning, framing, and waterproofing all have to agree. Our full build at 1502 Hancock Street sits a few blocks off Frankford Ave: custom kitchen, custom baths, and stairs our crew built on site.

Living through a renovation on a Fishtown block is its own logistics problem, and we plan for it: dumpster permits timed so they don't sit empty eating a parking spot for weeks, deliveries scheduled off the school-run hours on the narrow streets, and neighbors told what's coming before the demo starts. Most of our Fishtown work comes from referrals two doors down, which tells you how we treat the block.

[ Questions Fishtown Actually Asks ]

Fishtown Answers.

How much does it cost to renovate a rowhome in Fishtown?

Most Fishtown kitchen and bathroom remodels land between $25,000 and $60,000, and full gut renovations typically run $150,000 to $300,000 depending on size, structure, and finishes. Roof deck additions usually start around $30,000. Every estimate is free, itemized line by line, and based on walking your actual house — not a square-foot guess.

Do I need a permit to renovate a rowhome in Fishtown?

For anything structural, electrical, or plumbing — yes. Fishtown renovations run through Philadelphia L&I building permits with licensed-trade sign-offs and phased inspections. Cosmetic work like paint, flooring, and counters generally doesn't need one. We file and manage every permit as part of the job, and unpermitted work is a resale problem we won't build into your house.

How long does a Fishtown rowhome renovation take?

Kitchens run 3–10 weeks depending on scope, bathrooms 2–4 weeks, and a full gut renovation typically takes 4–6 months of construction after permits. Top-floor additions with roof decks add structural and zoning time up front. Your schedule is written into the proposal before we start, and you get an update every week.

Do you serve all of Fishtown and the 19125 ZIP code?

Yes — every block, river to Front Street, Girard to Lehigh, and the rest of 19125 with it. Our headquarters at 3146 Frankford Ave is minutes up the avenue, which means easy estimate scheduling and an owner who can be on your site quickly when something needs a second look.

Can you add a roof deck to my Fishtown rowhome?

Usually, yes — roof decks with pilot house access are Fishtown's signature project. It's structure-heavy work: the roof framing gets evaluated and often reinforced, zoning and permits get confirmed before design goes far, and waterproofing is treated as seriously as the framing. We handle the full scope from permit to final rail.

Do you handle party wall work and underpinning?

Yes. Fishtown rowhomes share structural brick walls with their neighbors, so beams, new pockets, and basement underpinning for headroom are engineered, permitted work — and coordination with the adjoining owner is part of doing it right. We treat shared walls with the seriousness two attached houses deserve.
