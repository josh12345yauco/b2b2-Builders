Building in Port Richmond

Port Richmond never emptied out the way some River Wards blocks did — its rowhomes stayed lived-in, often by the same families for two or three generations. That changes what "renovation" means here. The houses have good bones: solid brick, honest framing, roofs that were maintained because somebody's grandmother lived under them. The work isn't resurrection; it's respect.

Our bread-and-butter Port Richmond projects are kitchen and bath updates in houses that have earned them — modern function without erasing the character that makes these homes worth keeping. Alongside that: brick repointing where a century of weather has softened the mortar joints, and joist sistering where the old lumber needs a partner rather than a replacement. Ripping sound original material out of a Port Richmond rowhome to install something flimsier is the kind of flip-job disrespect that gets noticed on these blocks — and it should.

Port Richmond projects also come with a distinctly local constraint: the house is usually occupied, often by more than one generation, and moving out for a renovation isn't on the table. We sequence the work so the household keeps functioning — bathrooms switched over in a day, kitchens kept partially usable through demo, dust barriers treated as non-negotiable because someone's grandmother is on the other side of them. It's slower on paper and faster in reality, because a family that isn't miserable doesn't pause the job.

The new-construction story here is the river blocks. We designed and built five new homes at 3330-38 Salmon Street — each with its own kitchen, baths, and flooring, with exteriors that nod to the neighborhood's port history instead of ignoring it. A few doors down, our rehab at 3312 Salmon Street added an addition and a new facade to an existing home. Same street, two completely different disciplines — which is Port Richmond in a nutshell.

Port Richmond Answers.

How much does a rowhome update cost in Port Richmond?

Most Port Richmond kitchen remodels land between $25,000 and $60,000, bathrooms between $15,000 and $35,000, and a full gut renovation typically runs $150,000 to $300,000. Every estimate is free, itemized line by line, and priced from walking your actual house.

Do you serve Port Richmond?

Yes — heavily. We built five new homes at 3330-38 Salmon Street and rehabbed 3312 Salmon Street with an addition and facade changes. Port Richmond is a short drive up from our Frankford Ave headquarters.

Can you update my rowhome without making it look flipped?

That's exactly the assignment we like. Port Richmond rowhomes were built solidly, and many have been in the same family for generations. We repoint the brick instead of covering it, sister the joists instead of ripping them out, and build kitchens and baths that modernize the house without erasing it.

What are the most common projects you do in Port Richmond?

Kitchen and bathroom updates in well-kept family rowhomes, brick repointing and structural repairs, and new construction on the blocks near the river where lots have opened up. Multi-generation households also ask us for basement finishing and in-law setups.
