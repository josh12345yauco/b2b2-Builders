How Much Does a Kitchen Remodel Cost in Philadelphia? (2026 Real Numbers)

By the owner, B2B2 Builders · July 2026 · 7 min read

The quick answer: in Philadelphia in 2026, a pull-and-replace kitchen remodel typically runs $X–$X, a remodel with layout changes runs $X–$X, and a full gut with custom cabinetry runs $X+. Your specific number depends on three things: whether plumbing and gas move, what your cabinets are made of, and what a century-old rowhome is hiding under the floor.

We price kitchens across this city every week, and the honest truth is that most cost content online is national averages written by people who've never opened a Philadelphia rowhome wall. So here are the numbers the way we'd explain them at your kitchen table.

What Actually Moves the Price

1. Whether the plumbing and gas move. Keep the sink and stove where they are and you keep thousands in your pocket. Moving the sink means re-running supply and drain lines; moving a gas range means a new permitted gas line. This single decision separates the middle bands from the top ones.

2. Cabinets. They're usually the biggest single line item. Stock boxes, semi-custom, and full custom can differ by 3–4× for the same footprint. Semi-custom is the sweet spot for most Philadelphia homes — we break down the differences on our kitchen remodeling page.

3. What the house is hiding. This is the Philadelphia factor. Rowhome kitchens sit at the back of houses that are often 100+ years old — floors out of level, joists notched by generations of plumbers, sometimes knob-and-tube wiring behind the walls. A good local estimate carries realistic allowances for this; a national-franchise quote discovers it as change orders.

The Rowhome-Specific Factors

Three things we check on every Philadelphia kitchen that suburban cost guides never mention: floor structure (old joists may need sistering or reinforcement before they can carry tile and stone), the waste stack (if your kitchen drains into a century-old cast iron stack, replacing it while walls are open is cheap insurance), and wall condition (plaster-on-brick walls need furring before modern cabinets hang flat). None of these are deal-breakers — they're just real, and they belong in the estimate, not the surprise column.

When a Full Gut Makes Sense

If your kitchen needs three of these four — new layout, new systems, structural repair, and everything cosmetic — the math usually favors gutting the room once instead of renovating it twice. And if the whole house is at that point, a kitchen-only project may be the wrong unit of work entirely; read our guide on gut renovation vs. cosmetic remodel before you commit either way.
